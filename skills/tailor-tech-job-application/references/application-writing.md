# Application writing guide

Read this file before drafting the recruiter email, LinkedIn connection note, or cover letter.

## Shared narrative

All public-facing writing should answer three questions:

1. Why does this company's work matter to the candidate?
2. What role outcome can the candidate help create?
3. Which verified experience makes that contribution credible?

Use `company-context.md`, `job-analysis.md`, and `candidate-evidence.md` as the only factual inputs. Give each artifact a different job:

- The email earns a reply.
- The connection note earns permission to continue the conversation.
- The cover letter makes the full case for fit, contribution, and direction.

## Voice and anti-patterns

Use compact sentences, concrete nouns, and ordinary professional language. Sound confident, interested, and informed.

Avoid:

- em dashes;
- `not X but Y`, `not only X but also Y`, and similar staged contrasts;
- generic openings such as `I am writing to express my interest`;
- empty enthusiasm such as `amazing company`, `incredible opportunity`, or `dream role`;
- stock AI phrases such as `I hope this message finds you well`, `at the intersection of`, `deeply resonates`, `unique blend`, `fast-paced landscape`, or `delve`;
- adjective piles, inflated superlatives, and claims to be the best candidate;
- repeating the company's mission statement or the resume's bullet points;
- unsupported familiarity with internal plans, culture, or team dynamics;
- unnecessary use of the candidate's first-person pronoun at the start of consecutive sentences.

Read the prose aloud. Vary sentence length naturally. Replace abstractions with the actual product, customer, system, or engineering problem when the sources support it.

## Recruiter email

Maximum length: 200 words including the subject line. Prefer 90 to 160 words.

Use this shape when it fits:

1. A short subject tied to the role or contribution.
2. A direct opening naming the role and one specific company signal.
3. A contribution thesis about the problem the candidate can help solve.
4. One compact proof point from the resume, interpreted for this role rather than copied.
5. A clear, low-pressure request to discuss the position.

Do not narrate multiple achievements. Do not ask for a referral in the first cold email unless the user explicitly requests it. If the recruiter is unknown, use `Hello,` or `Hello recruiting team,` rather than inventing a name.

## LinkedIn connection note

Respect the current platform limit. If it cannot be verified, stay within 200 characters.

Use one company-specific reason and one credible point of professional overlap. Make the acceptance decision easy. Do not include a cover-letter summary, a resume link, a request for a referral, or a list of technologies.

A useful shape is:

`Hi <Name>, <specific reason the company's work is relevant>. My background in <credible overlap> makes the <role/team> especially interesting. I'd value connecting.`

Adapt the shape so it does not sound templated.

## Cover letter

Keep the letter to one page, usually 300 to 500 words. Use three to five short paragraphs.

### Opening

Name the role and lead with a specific company or product problem. State the candidate's contribution thesis early. Skip ceremonial language.

### Main contribution case

Use one or two examples that prove the capabilities most central to the job. Explain the connection to the employer's needs. Do not retell the full resume.

Cover:

- why the past experience transfers to this role;
- how the candidate approaches the relevant technical or organizational problem;
- the distinctive quality the candidate brings, supported by evidence;
- the likely contribution to the team or product.

Demonstrate unusually strong fit through specificity, judgment, and evidence. Avoid unverifiable ranking language such as `best suited` or `top candidate`.

### Growth fit

Use a smaller portion of the letter to explain why this role is a logical next step. Connect near-term growth to the scope of the role and long-term growth to plausible paths inside the company. Do not assert promotion timelines or organizational structures that sources do not establish.

Keep this section employer-aware: the growth sought should improve the candidate's ability to contribute.

### Close

Reinforce the contribution thesis, invite a conversation, and end directly. Avoid pleading, vague hope, or repeated enthusiasm.

## Quality test

After drafting, remove any sentence that could be sent unchanged to five different companies. Every remaining company-specific sentence must be traceable to `company-context.md`, and every candidate claim must be traceable to `candidate-evidence.md`.

## Basis for the structure

The contribution-first structure follows established university career guidance that a cover letter should connect a small number of relevant examples to the employer's stated needs instead of summarizing the resume:

- [Harvard Mignone Center: Create Impactful Resumes and Cover Letters](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/hes-create-impactful-resumes-and-cover-letters/)
- [Harvard Mignone Center: Create a Resume/CV or Cover Letter](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/channels/create-a-resume-cv-or-cover-letter/)
- [MIT UPOP Cover Letter Guide](https://upop.mit.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/UPOP_Cover_Letter_Guide.pdf)

Treat these as durable principles. Browse again if the user asks for current platform limits or current hiring conventions.
