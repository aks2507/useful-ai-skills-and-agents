# Company research and context dossier

Read this file for every application.

## Research standard

Use live web research. The goal is a decision-ready picture of the company and the role, grounded in trustworthy sources and saved before any outreach is drafted.

Start with these source tiers:

1. **First-party:** company home, product pages, documentation, engineering blog, security or trust center, newsroom, careers pages, leadership statements, and the job posting.
2. **Primary public records:** regulator filings, official corporate registries, government publications, patents when directly relevant, and published earnings materials.
3. **Reliable independent reporting:** established business or technical publications that identify their evidence and distinguish reporting from opinion.
4. **Named partner and customer sources:** case studies or announcements that establish a concrete relationship. Treat promotional claims cautiously.

Do not use social-media rumors, anonymous posts, Glassdoor, Blind, Reddit, employee-review sites, scraped content farms, or unattributed summaries. A first-party claim can establish what the company says; it does not automatically prove market success. Corroborate consequential claims when possible.

Prefer direct pages over search-result snippets. Record publication or update dates. For sources without a visible date, record the access date.

## Coverage

Answer the questions that affect the application:

- What does the company build or sell?
- Who uses it, buys it, or benefits from it?
- How does the company appear to create value or earn revenue?
- What technical systems, workflows, or constraints are central to the business?
- What recent launches, investments, partnerships, filings, or leadership statements reveal current priorities?
- Which customer, scale, reliability, security, cost, adoption, regulatory, or operational problems is it addressing?
- What does the job description suggest this team must deliver now?
- Which parts of the candidate's background can plausibly help?
- What remains unknown?

For a private company, do not guess financial performance, headcount, funding, or market share. For a public company, use current filings for material financial or strategic claims.

Continue until research reaches practical saturation: two successive trustworthy sources add no material role-relevant information. This is a stopping rule, not a claim that the internet was exhaustively searched.

## Writing the dossier

Write factual statements with inline citations close to the claim. Mark analysis with language such as `Inference:` or `Role implication:`. A strong dossier makes it easy to distinguish:

- what the company explicitly states;
- what an independent source corroborates;
- what the job posting signals;
- what Codex infers from those facts.

Do not copy marketing slogans into the application. Translate verified context into a concrete statement about the work, user, constraint, or outcome that matters.

Keep durable business context separate from time-sensitive developments. This makes the file useful later while showing which facts need to be refreshed.
